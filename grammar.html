Grammatik-Spickzettel

The rules you need to read stories in German, explained simply. Most examples are real sentences from Die Verwandlung. You don't have to learn all of them at once: the reader shows you which ones a sentence uses.

Verbzweitstellung

The verb is in position 2

In a normal sentence, the conjugated verb is always the second element. If something other than the subject comes first (a time, a place, a whole side clause…), the subject moves behind the verb.

Er lag auf seinem Rücken.He lay on his back. (subject first)
Dann lag er auf seinem Rücken.Then he lay on his back. (“Dann” first → er moves after the verb)
Als Gregor eines Morgens erwachte, fand er sich verwandelt.The whole side clause is “position 1”, so the main clause starts with the verb: fand er.

English does this only in questions. In German, whenever you see “verb + subject”, look at what came first.

Nebensatz

Side clauses: the verb goes to the end

Words like als, wenn, dass (daß), weil, ob, obwohl, während, nachdem, bevor, bis, damit start a side clause. Inside it, the conjugated verb waits until the very end. Tip: read to the end of the clause before you try to understand it.

Relativsatz

der / die / das = “which, who, that”

After a comma, der, die, das, dem, den, dessen, deren often don't mean “the” but “which / who / that”. They describe the word just before the comma, and the verb goes to the end.

…hing das Bild, das er vor kurzem aus einer illustrierten Zeitschrift ausgeschnitten und in einem hübschen, vergoldeten Rahmen untergebracht hatte.…hung the picture that he had recently cut out of an illustrated magazine and put into a nice gilded frame.

How to tell: “the” is followed by a noun (das Bild). “which” is followed by other words and the clause ends with a verb.

Präteritum

The story past tense

Books tell stories in the Präteritum (simple past). In speech, Germans mostly use the Perfekt instead, which is why these forms feel new. The most important ones are irregular, so learn them like vocabulary:

Regular verbs just add -te: erwachen → erwachte, versuchen → versuchte.

»Was ist mit mir geschehen?« dachte er. Es war kein Traum.“What has happened to me?” he thought. It was no dream.
Perfekt · Plusquamperfekt

haben / sein + Partizip II

Perfekt = present of haben/sein + participle at the end: “has done / did”.
Plusquamperfekt = hatte/war + participle: “had done”, for things that happened before the story's moment.

»Was ist mit mir geschehen?«“What has happened to me?” (Perfekt)
…das er vor kurzem aus einer illustrierten Zeitschrift ausgeschnitten und in einem hübschen, vergoldeten Rahmen untergebracht hatte.…which he had recently cut out of a magazine and put into a frame. (Plusquamperfekt)
Partizip I · Partizip II

Participles

Partizip II (ge…t / ge…en): gemacht, gesehen, verwandelt. It's used for the past (with haben/sein), for the passive (with werden), or as an adjective.
Partizip I (infinitive + d): zappelnd = wriggling. Used like an adjective, so it gets endings.

…fand er sich zu einem Ungeziefer verwandelt.…he found himself transformed into a vermin. (Partizip II as a description)
…um die zappelnden Beine nicht sehen zu müssen…so as not to have to see the wriggling legs (Partizip I + ending)
Trennbare Verben

Separable verbs: the front part jumps to the end

Many verbs have a front part: aufstehen, zurückschaukeln, ansehen. In a main clause, that front part goes to the end of the clause. If a short word like auf, an, aus, zurück, ein, mit, vor, weg sits at the end of a clause, glue it back onto the verb before you look it up!

…immer wieder schaukelte er in die Rückenlage zurück.…again and again he rocked back onto his back. → zurück|schaukeln
Er stand auf.He got up. → auf|stehen
Modalverben

können, müssen, wollen, sollen, dürfen, mögen

A modal verb sits in position 2. The other verb goes to the end in its basic form (infinitive).

…konnte sich aber in seinem gegenwärtigen Zustand nicht in diese Lage bringen.…but in his present state he could not get himself into this position.

zu + Infinitiv

zu + infinitive = “to …”

zu + verb at the end of a clause = “to do”. With um … zu it means “in order to”, with ohne … zu “without …-ing”.

Er schloß die Augen, um die zappelnden Beine nicht sehen zu müssen.He closed his eyes in order not to have to see the wriggling legs.
Die vier Fälle

Cases: why “der” keeps changing

Articles change depending on the job of the noun in the sentence. This table is the key to half of German:

masculinefeminineneuterplural
Nominativ (subject)der / eindie / einedas / eindie
Akkusativ (direct object)den / einendie / einedas / eindie
Dativ (to/for, after mit, von, zu, aus, bei, nach…)dem / einemder / einerdem / einemden (+ -n)
Genitiv (“of”)des / eines (+ -s)der / einerdes / eines (+ -s)der

Same pattern for mein, sein, ihr, kein: in seinem Bett = in his bed (Dativ after in, saying where).

eines Morgens … aus unruhigen Träumenone morning (Genitiv of time) … from troubled dreams (Dativ plural after “aus”)
Adjektivendungen

Adjective endings

An adjective before a noun gets an ending. The simple rule that covers most cases in books:

  • After der/die/das (and dies-, jen-, welch-): usually -e or -en.
  • In the Dativ, Genitiv and plural after an article: almost always -en.
  • With no article, the adjective shows the case itself: schwerer Rücken, liebes Kind.
auf seinem panzerartig harten Rückenon his armour-like hard back (Dativ → -en)

For reading, endings rarely change the meaning. Ignore them at first, and look up the word without the ending.

Passiv

werden + Partizip II = something is done

The passive uses werden (wird / wurde) + Partizip II at the end: the focus is on what happens, not who does it.

Die Tür wurde noch mit dem Stock zugeschlagen.The door was then slammed shut with the stick.
Konjunktiv II

The “would / could” form

Used for wishes, unreal situations and politeness. The forms look like the Präteritum, often with an umlaut: war → wäre, hatte → hätte, konnte → könnte, vergaß → vergäße. Or simply würde + infinitive.

»Wie wäre es, wenn ich noch ein wenig weiterschliefe und alle Narrheiten vergäße,« dachte er.“How would it be if I slept a little longer and forgot all this nonsense,” he thought.
